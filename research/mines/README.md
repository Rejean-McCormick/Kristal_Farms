# Mine-reuse research

This workspace explores whether existing mining assets in northern Québec and Labrador can reduce the cost, schedule or physical exposure of Kristal Farms infrastructure.

**Research status:** exploratory only. Nothing in this directory selects or ranks a site.

## Two distinct hypotheses

### 1. Underground / industrial infrastructure reuse

Recently closed, suspended or care-and-maintenance mines may retain valuable infrastructure such as ramps, shafts, caverns, substations, switchgear, pumping, ventilation, communications, workshops, roads, camps and airstrips.

For this use case, **recency matters** because dewatering, ground-support inspection, power systems and other assets can deteriorate or be dismantled after closure. A shutdown within roughly five years is therefore a useful research preference, not a universal eligibility rule.

Formal project language should use **subsurface or hardened compute infrastructure**. The shorthand “bunker” does not establish blast resistance, security certification, survivability or fitness for data-center occupation.

### 2. Mine-pit reservoir / pumped-storage reuse

Open pits may be investigated as upper or lower reservoirs for pumped-storage hydropower (PSH/STEP). This includes:

- paired mine pits;
- a mine pit paired with a purpose-built freshwater reservoir;
- a mine pit paired with an appropriate existing water body, subject to environmental review;
- a coastal elevated mine pit paired with the ocean as the lower reservoir.

For reservoir reuse, **mine age is not a disqualifier**. Older mines can be viable if pit geometry, head, usable volume, hydrology, geotechnical stability, water chemistry, sealing requirements, environmental liabilities, rights/governance and grid value are acceptable.
