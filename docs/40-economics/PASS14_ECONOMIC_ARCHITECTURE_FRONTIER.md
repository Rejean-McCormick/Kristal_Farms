# Pass 14 — Economic architecture frontier

## Conclusion

The available evidence supports a **structural research thesis**, not a bankable savings claim. Completed Québec 735-kV projects imply project-total/line-length ratios of roughly **C$2.96M to C$4.85M per line-km**, with materially different scope. The publicly tabled North Labrador road study estimates roughly **C$2.60M/km** before paving and **C$3.34M/km** including the stated paving increment. Northern fibre funding decisions imply much smaller funding-per-km ratios, roughly **C$85k to C$209k/km**, but those are funding proxies rather than total fibre construction costs.

This difference is large enough to justify project-level testing of Kristal Farms' central inversion: **bring flexible compute to new remote generation and export digital value by fibre instead of automatically building long road + HV corridors.**

It does not establish that Kristal is cheaper at every site.

## 64-case distance frontier

The non-site stress grid spans:

- HV export: 25 / 50 / 100 / 200 km;
- export road: 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 km;
- fibre: 100 / 250 / 500 / 933 km.

Using the lower transmission/road reference against the higher fibre proxy, **60/64** cases leave positive budget headroom before all unpriced Kristal-specific infrastructure. **4/64** do not. Under the opposite reference combination, **64/64** are positive.

This is intentionally not presented as a probability or success rate. The 64 combinations are generic stress cases, not a statistical sample of northern sites.

## What remains unpriced

- serviced compute pads;
- local MV/substation/switchgear;
- port upgrades;
- fibre shore-end/landing/terminal;
- local access road;
- storage/backup if required;
- heat recovery where justified;
- full operations and maintenance;
- taxes/financing/contingency;
- transmission losses and grid reinforcement;
- project-specific environmental/rights costs.

Therefore the calculated positive difference is called **remaining unpriced Kristal budget**, not savings.

## Next mode of work

Pass 14 ends broad research. Future economics should be built inside **corridor/site dossiers** with real distances, real hydro engineering, real port/fibre quotations, community governance, environmental constraints and current commercial terms.
