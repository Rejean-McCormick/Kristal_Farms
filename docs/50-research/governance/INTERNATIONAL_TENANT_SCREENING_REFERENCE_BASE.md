# International Tenant Screening — Reference Base

**As of:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Research/reference layer (C4). Not legal advice and not an automatic eligibility engine.

## Purpose

This reference note supplies external starting points for the Kristal Farms international counterparty policy. It supports review; it does not replace explicit project decisions or transaction-specific legal advice.

## Responsible business conduct

### UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights

Official OHCHR publication:  
https://www.ohchr.org/documents/publications/guidingprinciplesbusinesshr_en.pdf

Relevant themes for Kristal Farms include corporate responsibility to respect human rights, risk-based due diligence and access to remedy.

### OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct

Official OECD publication:  
https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-guidelines-for-multinational-enterprises-on-responsible-business-conduct_81f92357-en.html

The 2023 Guidelines cover human rights, labour rights, environment, bribery, disclosure, science/technology, competition, taxation and supply-chain due diligence.

### OECD AI Principles

Official OECD reference:  
https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/ai-principles.html

Relevant themes include human rights and democratic values, privacy, robustness/security/safety, transparency appropriate to role and accountability.

These principles are useful when evaluating the institutional posture of AI counterparties. They do **not** imply that Kristal Farms can inspect private tenant workloads.

## Canadian sanctions and trade controls

### Consolidated Canadian Autonomous Sanctions List

Official Global Affairs Canada reference:  
https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/international_relations-relations_internationales/sanctions/consolidated-consolide.aspx?lang=eng

Use as an administrative screening aid only. The underlying regulations control and must be checked for transaction-specific legal effect.

### Canada's Export Control List

Official Global Affairs Canada guide:  
https://www.international.gc.ca/trade-commerce/controls-controles/ecl-lec/index.aspx?lang=eng

Advanced computing hardware, technical data or other controlled items may create obligations separate from tenant-eligibility policy. Technology-origin rules must therefore be reviewed separately from the current exclusion of United States counterparties.

## Human-rights reference layer

See `HUMAN_RIGHTS_REFERENCE_BASE.md` for UDHR, ICCPR, ICESCR, UNDRIP and CRC starting points.

Country-level human-rights, democracy, corruption or civic-space indices may be used as **research signals** but must not be treated as automatic eligibility determinations. Prefer multiple sources, primary/legal materials where available, dated evidence and written reasoning.

## Screening-source hierarchy

For a real counterparty review, prefer:

1. applicable statutes, regulations, sanctions orders and official government lists;
2. company registries, securities filings and verified beneficial-ownership/control records;
3. court/regulatory findings and enforcement decisions;
4. intergovernmental and recognized human-rights/responsible-business sources;
5. audited corporate disclosures and credible investigative reporting;
6. secondary indices and NGO assessments as contextual signals;
7. social media and unverified allegations only as leads requiring corroboration.

## Non-automation rule

No external index should be copied into a single numeric Kristal Farms "ethics score." The correct output is a documented categorical state — `ELIGIBLE`, `ENHANCED_DUE_DILIGENCE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `INELIGIBLE` — attached to a named legal counterparty, its ownership/control structure, evidence and date.
