# Human Rights Reference Base for the Human Dignity Framework

**As of:** 2026-08-17  
**Status:** External reference note; not legal advice and not a determination of which legal obligations apply to a specific Kristal Farms program.

The project-level Human Dignity Framework uses internationally recognized human-rights principles as a design baseline while requiring jurisdiction-specific legal and community review before implementation.

## Primary reference instruments

### Universal Declaration of Human Rights (United Nations)

Official source: https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights

Relevant design themes include inherent dignity and equality, freedom of thought/conscience/religion, education directed toward human development and respect for rights, and participation in cultural life and scientific advancement.

### International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (OHCHR)

Official source: https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html

Relevant design themes include non-discrimination, equality before the law, freedom of religion/belief, expression, association, movement and the protection of minority cultural, religious and linguistic life.

### International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (OHCHR)

Official source: https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICESCR.html

Relevant design themes include non-discrimination and equal enjoyment of economic, social and cultural rights, including education and cultural participation.

### United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (United Nations)

Official source: https://www.un.org/development/desa/indigenouspeoples/declaration-on-the-rights-of-indigenous-peoples.html

Relevant design themes include Indigenous peoples' institutions, culture, language, education, participation, rights associated with lands/resources and the importance of free, prior and informed consent in relevant contexts.

### Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNICEF / United Nations system)

Official source: https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text

Relevant design themes include non-discrimination, best interests and safeguarding, freedom of thought/conscience/religion, education, respect for identity/language/cultural values, minority/Indigenous cultural and linguistic rights, recreation and protection from exploitation.

## Repository use rule

These instruments are a **normative reference base**, not a shortcut around local law or Indigenous/community rights. Before a real program launches, legal counsel and affected rights holders must identify the applicable legal instruments, public-language rules, education/accreditation requirements, safeguarding obligations, privacy requirements and complaint/remedy mechanisms.
