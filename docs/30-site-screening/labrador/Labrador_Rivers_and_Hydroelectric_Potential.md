# **Labrador Rivers and Hydroelectric Potential: Power Estimates and Current Status**

## Scope and status categories

This document lists Labrador rivers and watercourses for which public sources identify hydroelectric potential, existing hydroelectric development, or small-hydro study work. The figures combine several kinds of data: historical potential estimates, preliminary site estimates, current installed capacity, and small-hydro feasibility figures. Values are therefore not always directly comparable.

**Status categories used:**

- **Operating / harnessed:** an existing hydroelectric station is operating.

- **Partially harnessed:** part of the river system is developed, but additional identified potential remains.

- **Planned / under agreement:** development is identified in a current agreement or official proposal, but not yet operating.

- **Former / retired:** a hydro station existed but is no longer operating as an independent facility.

- **Unexploited / identified:** hydro potential has been identified, but no current operating hydro station was confirmed.

- **Small-hydro candidate:** a small local hydro option was studied, but no operating project was confirmed.

Primary sources used include: Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro’s current generation-asset list, which identifies Churchill Falls, Muskrat Falls and Menihek as Labrador hydro facilities; the 2024 Newfoundland and Labrador–Québec energy partnership materials, which identify Gull Island, a new Churchill Falls powerhouse and Churchill Falls upgrades; the 1974 historical Labrador potential table; the 2002 preliminary site list reproduced in the Royal Commission Churchill River report; and the 2018 Coastal Labrador small-hydro reassessment. ([<u>Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro</u>](https://nlhydro.com/about-us/our-electricity-system/our-generation-assets/))

## Key caveats

The strongest current-status evidence is for **Churchill Falls, Muskrat Falls and Menihek**, because they are listed by Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro as operating Labrador hydro assets. ([<u>Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro</u>](https://nlhydro.com/about-us/our-electricity-system/our-generation-assets/))

The strongest current planned-development evidence is for **Gull Island**, **Churchill Falls expansion**, and **Churchill Falls upgrades**, because they are described in the 2024 official NL–Québec energy partnership materials as new developments or capacity increases, but the same source says the MOU is non-binding until a definitive agreement is reached. ([<u>Government of Newfoundland and Labrador</u>](https://www.gov.nl.ca/releases/2024/exec/1212n01/))

The remaining large-river figures are mostly **historical or preliminary potential estimates**, not evidence of current projects. The 2002 list explicitly warns that the site capacities are approximate and could change significantly after further study. ([<u>Government of Newfoundland and Labrador</u>](https://www.gov.nl.ca/publicat/royalcomm/research/Churchill.pdf))
