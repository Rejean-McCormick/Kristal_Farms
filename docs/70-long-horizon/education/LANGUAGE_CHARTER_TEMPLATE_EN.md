## 3. Teaching and working languages

Document additional languages used for:

- technical instruction;
- university/academic instruction;
- scientific publication;
- tenant/industry operations;
- international student services;
- emergency response;
- health services;
- legal/administrative notices.

For each language, state whether it is an official local institutional language, teaching language, working language, translation language, or service-access language.

## 4. Translation and interpretation as infrastructure

Define:

- minimum interpretation coverage;
- translation turnaround standards;
- emergency/health interpretation availability;
- terminology/glossary governance;
- technical-document translation workflow;
- machine-translation uses and human-review requirements;
- accessibility support.

## 5. Language transmission and education

Define how the institution will invest in host-language continuity:

- early-childhood / school support where relevant;
- adult learning;
- technical vocabulary development;
- teacher/instructor development;
- digital corpora, keyboards, fonts, speech tools and educational resources;
- cultural archives controlled with community consent;
- scholarships or paid language-learning time.

## 6. International participants

Specify:

- minimum orientation to host language/culture;
- what level, if any, is required for particular programs or community-facing roles;
- what services remain accessible before proficiency is reached;
- how additional languages support international learning without displacing the host language.

## 7. Individual rights

No person should lose access to essential health, safety, grievance, legal, safeguarding or emergency information because of language ability.

No language requirement should be used as a disguised barrier unrelated to a legitimate role or learning objective.

## 9. Acceptance gate

The charter is not active until:

- host-community/rights-holder governance has approved it;
- applicable language/education/employment/accessibility law has been reviewed;
- translation capacity is budgeted;
- emergency and health language access is operational;
- complaint and appeal routes are independent and understandable;
- the Human Dignity Framework review is complete.
