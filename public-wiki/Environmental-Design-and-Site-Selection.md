# Environmental Design and Site Selection

Kristal Farms does not treat "the North" as one ecological category. Some northern areas are highly sensitive, protected or culturally important. The project documentation already identifies candidate rivers and areas that should be excluded or heavily constrained for those reasons.

## Preferred siting principles

Where practical, the project favours:

- existing or previously disturbed industrial or port land;
- compact modular yards;
- short local electrical routes;
- minimal unnecessary corridor clearing;
- reduced need for new inland roads through marine logistics;
- closed-loop, non-contact cooling;
- no evaporative cooling towers;
- heat reuse before rejection;
- reversible equipment and restoration planning;
- explicit fish, habitat, water, cultural and rights-holder review.

## Why not say "low biodiversity"?

Low species count is not the same as low ecological importance. Arctic and subarctic ecosystems can be highly sensitive, slow to recover and culturally significant.

The public criterion is therefore **low ecological sensitivity and low habitat conflict**, not a generic claim of low biodiversity.

## Site exclusion is part of the model

A river or site should be rejected if protected-area status, fish habitat, cultural use, rights, construction burden or other impacts make the project inappropriate.

Environmental discipline is not a mitigation step added after site selection. It is part of the selection algorithm.
