# Structural Cost Advantage

The economic documentation describes Kristal Farms as a **cost-avoidance infrastructure model** rather than a project whose only advantage is cheap electricity.

The intended advantage comes from reducing several major cost drivers at the same time.

## Cost drivers the model seeks to avoid or reduce

1. **Long-distance high-voltage transmission** — fewer long lines, remote substations, rights-of-way and line losses.
2. **Major new access roads** — coastal logistics and modular delivery can reduce heavy inland road requirements.
3. **Mechanical cooling infrastructure** — cold climate, liquid cooling and non-contact heat exchange can reduce cooling overhead.
4. **Heat rejection** — recovered heat becomes useful local energy instead of a pure disposal cost.
5. **Large permanent data-centre buildings** — standardized containerized pads allow staged deployment.
6. **Speculative overbuilding** — capacity can be added pad by pad as tenants, power, fibre and heat sinks are confirmed.
7. **Urban land and some physical-security burden** — remote or port-adjacent industrial locations can have different land and perimeter economics.
8. **Diesel heating** — useful server heat can offset part of local fossil-fuel demand where the heat network supports it.

## Why the wiki uses strong language

Kristal Farms is not designed to compete on every possible site. It is designed to **select sites where these advantages stack together**. For that target class, the project objective is a significant structural advantage in total infrastructure cost and footprint.

That is stronger than saying the model is merely "sometimes useful." It is also more precise than saying it is automatically superior everywhere.

## Numbers are still site-specific

The supplied studies use different transmission benchmarks for different comparison purposes. They demonstrate that long northern transmission can be extremely expensive, but they are not interchangeable project estimates. See [[Working Economic Examples]] for the current ranges and caveats.
