# Cold Climate Cooling

Cooling is one of the major infrastructure and operating burdens of a data centre. Kristal Farms treats the northern climate as part of the cooling system.

## Baseline architecture

The detailed community-integrated design uses:

- liquid or rear-door heat capture;
- a closed IT cooling loop;
- plate heat exchangers;
- a separate village heat loop;
- non-contact exchange with cold seawater or bay water where appropriate;
- backup dry coolers;
- no routine mixing between IT coolant, building-loop water and environmental water.

## Why this matters economically

The supplied Canadian data-centre cost study estimates complete cooling — capital, energy and maintenance — at roughly **8–15% of total cost** in a modern centre, potentially higher for some high-density AI configurations.

The project economic model therefore treats cold climate as a structural cost advantage, not simply an operating convenience.

## PUE target

The working economic model studies a future Kristal Farms PUE target around **1.10–1.20**, subject to engineering validation. This should not be read as an achieved operating result.

## Water

The design avoids evaporative cooling towers and uses closed loops, so the intended consumptive water use for cooling is near zero except for minor system top-ups. Environmental heat rejection still requires temperature limits, monitoring and site-specific approval.
