# Transmission vs Digital Export

The central infrastructure substitution in Kristal Farms is:

> **Move bits over fibre instead of moving bulk electricity over very long distances.**

## Electrical export can require

- high-voltage transmission lines;
- substations and transformation equipment;
- rights-of-way and corridor clearing;
- access roads and construction camps;
- river and terrain crossings;
- long permitting schedules;
- long-term line inspection and maintenance;
- electrical losses over distance.

## Local compute requires

- local generation or a nearby renewable connection;
- a short local feeder and substation;
- compute pads;
- cooling and heat infrastructure;
- a reliable fibre path;
- network operations and repair capability.

The comparison is therefore not "a transmission line versus nothing." It is **one complete infrastructure pathway versus another**.

## Working cost evidence in the repository

The project documentation contains two useful but different transmission benchmarks:

- a northern Canadian transmission benchmark of roughly **C$1.09 million/km** derived from a large northern Ontario project;
- a Québec 735 kV benchmark of roughly **C$5 million/km** derived from the Micoua–Saguenay line for a simplified Nunavik screening exercise.

The Québec screening explicitly excludes substations, local connections, roads, camps, studies, environmental/territorial compensation, contingencies, financing and network reinforcement. It is therefore a **comparison benchmark**, not a project quotation.

The strategic conclusion is robust even though exact costs vary: **distance can turn electrical export into a very large infrastructure program.** Kristal Farms exists to test whether a digital-export architecture is a better use of selected remote resources.
