# Communities, Languages and Dignity

The project vision supports cultural plurality without treating culture as a reason to reduce individual freedom.

## Host communities first

Existing Indigenous peoples, municipalities, residents and rights holders must have meaningful decision rights over local expansion. No project should treat inhabited or culturally used land as empty simply because population density is low.

## Language

The long-term education/community vision proposes site-level language charters in which the host people's language or languages can hold primary local institutional standing, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law and as determined with the host people.

Additional working languages can support international science, technical documentation and exchange. Translation and interpretation are treated as infrastructure rather than an afterthought.

## Culture, rites and customs

Communities can preserve and develop language, food, dress, ceremonies, religion, arts, celebrations and social institutions.

The boundary is individual dignity and consent. No project-controlled custom or rite can justify coercion, violence, discrimination, forced participation, denial of education or health care, restriction of lawful movement or loss of independent remedy.

The dual principle is:

> **Protect communities from forced assimilation, and protect individuals from coercion inside any community or institution.**
