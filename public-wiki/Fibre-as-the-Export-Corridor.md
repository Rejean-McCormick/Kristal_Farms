# Fibre as the Export Corridor

Fibre is not a secondary utility in Kristal Farms. It is the infrastructure that exports the site's economic output.

## Why fibre is central

The project does not primarily sell electricity to distant markets. It sells compute services and digital results. Reliable connectivity therefore performs the role that a long electrical export corridor performs in a conventional remote-energy model.

## Baseline connectivity design

The partner architecture includes:

- a network operations centre (NOC);
- high-capacity regional trunk fibre;
- dual A/B pad uplinks;
- path diversity where feasible;
- automatic failover;
- monitoring of uptime, latency, jitter and errors;
- network separation between tenant and community traffic;
- no deep packet inspection of tenant payloads.

## Coastal and subsea opportunity

Coastal sites may eventually support:

- marine landing points;
- subsea fibre routes;
- coastal rings;
- multiple northern compute nodes linked by resilient paths.

These are strategic opportunities, not yet established assets. Specific seabed routes, permits, landing rights, carrier agreements, latency and cost must be validated separately.

## The long-term idea

A collection of successful nodes could eventually support a **northern fibre crown**: a resilient network that connects energy-rich compute sites and strengthens regional communications at the same time.
