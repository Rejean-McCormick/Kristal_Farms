# Why the North

Kristal Farms is designed around conditions that can be disadvantages for conventional infrastructure but advantages for this model.

## Cold

Cold air and cold coastal water can reduce the mechanical effort required to remove heat from high-density compute. The baseline design uses liquid cooling, closed loops and non-contact heat exchangers rather than relying on large evaporative cooling towers.

Cold also creates real demand for recovered heat. A megawatt of computing power eventually becomes roughly a megawatt of heat, so a northern site can use what another data centre may need to discard.

## Remote renewable resources

Some rivers and renewable resources are far from major electrical load centres. Their energy may be technically valuable but expensive to monetize if the first requirement is a major transmission build-out.

Kristal Farms changes the export product from **electricity** to **digital work**.

## Coastal access

Many target communities are coastal. Marine access can support delivery of standardized containers, electrical skids, piping and other modules while reducing reliance on long new inland roads.

Coastal geography may also create future opportunities for marine or subsea fibre, although specific routes and landing rights still require engineering, carrier, rights-holder and regulatory validation.

## Space — but not empty space

Northern territory is not treated as unused simply because population density is low. Site screening must account for ecological sensitivity, protected areas, fishing, traditional land use, rights, cultural value and community decisions.

The project preference is for **compact sites with low ecological conflict, previously disturbed industrial/port land where practical, and minimal unnecessary new corridors**.
