# Working Economic Examples

The repository contains several illustrative models. They help explain scale, but they are **not construction bids or investment-grade estimates**.

## Transmission

Two source exercises use different benchmarks:

| Working benchmark | Approximate value | Intended use |
|---|---:|---|
| Northern Ontario transmission comparison | ~C$1.09M/km | Broad Canadian avoided-transmission illustration |
| Québec 735 kV comparison | ~C$5M/km | Simplified west-Nunavik long-distance screening |

The Québec exercise explicitly excludes substations, roads, camps, studies, environmental/territorial compensation, contingencies, financing and reinforcement of the existing southern network.

## Roads

The working economic model cites an illustrative northern all-season-road benchmark around **C$3M/km**. Actual cost depends heavily on terrain, bridges, permafrost, material sources, construction season and logistics.

## Cooling

The supplied Canadian data-centre study estimates:

- complete cooling around **8–15% of total cost** for a modern centre;
- potentially **10–20%** for some high-density configurations;
- cooling electricity alone as a smaller subset of total cost.

## PUE

The economic model studies a future Kristal Farms target around **1.10–1.20**, while using conventional comparison cases around the mid-1.4 range. This is a target assumption, not measured project performance.

## Heat

The economic model uses illustrative HUF scenarios of 50%, 70% and 90% to show how useful heat can offset fuel. Actual heat value depends on building demand, network losses, temperature, boiler efficiency, seasonal demand and storage.

## The correct interpretation

The numbers are best used to show **why site-specific comparison matters**. The project thesis is that a qualifying Kristal Farms site should show large stacked savings — not that one generic C$/km figure applies everywhere.
