# Project Direction — International Mobilization and Distributed Resilience

**Captured:** 2026-08-31  
**Origin:** direction supplied by the founder and sole member of Initiative kOA for the Kristal Farms project.  
**Authority:** owner/founder project direction; authoritative for intended strategy, not independent evidence of market demand, government support, site feasibility, diplomatic commitment or security guarantee.

Core direction:

- develop **new renewable generation** for Kristal Farms rather than attaching the commercial model to existing dams; an existing grid or generating asset may be used temporarily for construction where lawful and practical, but it is not the long-term resource thesis;
- consume the new generation **locally at the compute site**, minimizing long-distance electrical export infrastructure;
- prioritize coastal or marine-accessible site ensembles with practical heavy-cargo access, high-bandwidth telecom potential and comparatively low ecological sensitivity, subject to site-specific biodiversity, rights/governance and environmental review;
- use staged construction power where useful — for example wind plus temporary thermal backup, followed by a small new hydro project that can support construction of a larger hydro project or a cascade of smaller projects — without treating that sequence as mandatory where another low-impact sequence is superior;
- provide standard physical and telecom interfaces; do **not** invent a proprietary Kristal Farms data-transport format, protocol or shared tenant data plane;
- allow eligible international tenants to install and control their own servers, containers, modules, racks or other contracted compute systems; tenant control of hardware, software, data and cryptographic keys remains within the black-box/content-blind tenancy boundary and applicable Canadian law;
- build an international portfolio across multiple eligible organizations and jurisdictions so that no single customer, country, carrier, port, site or technology vendor becomes the sole point of commercial dependence;
- treat international diversification as a **resilience layer**: a serious disruption could affect several independent foreign commercial stakeholders and therefore create broader incentives for continuity, restoration and diplomatic attention;
- do not describe that resilience as a military alliance, collective-defence guarantee, extraterritorial status or immunity from Canadian law. The statement “attacking Kristal Farms attacks a coalition of powers” is a strategic intuition to be translated into evidence-backed commercial/diplomatic resilience, not asserted literally as a treaty obligation;
- first validate demand and institutional appetite through non-binding requirements exchanges, letters of interest and government/carrier feasibility discussions before presenting a 12-member coalition as established.

The intended mobilization sequence is:

> **international demand signal → Québec/Canada coordination → telecom and marine feasibility → site dossiers → pilot → replicated multi-country portfolio**
