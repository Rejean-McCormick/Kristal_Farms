# 01_Partner_Ask_and_Roles

**Project:** Kristal Farms — Labrador Coast Partner Package  
**Focus:** Nain first; Labrador coastal replication second  
**Document status:** Partner-facing working draft  
**Intended audience:** Utilities, infrastructure investors, compute tenants, modular data center suppliers, telecom/fiber partners, community partners, and government partners  

---

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document clarifies what Kristal Farms is asking from each type of partner.

Kristal Farms is not presenting a generic data center concept. The project thesis is specific: place modular compute containers near Labrador coastal communities where cold hydro, cold ambient conditions, village heat demand, marine logistics, and fiber connectivity can be combined into one community-scale infrastructure model.

The first target is **Nain**. The expansion logic is replication across other Labrador coastal communities where the same conditions can be validated.

The partner opportunity is to help turn this model from a defined concept into a validated pilot: local hydro-connected compute pads, short medium-voltage electrical connection, village heat reuse, black-box tenancy, fiber export of compute results, and community-centered governance.

---

## 2. What Kristal Farms is asking for now

At this stage, Kristal Farms is seeking **partner alignment and due diligence participation**, not a final construction commitment.

The immediate ask is for partners to help validate six things:

1. **Power feasibility** — whether a local hydro-connected supply can support staged compute pads while protecting local needs.
2. **Village siting feasibility** — whether containers can be placed near heat users, preferably near port or village-edge infrastructure.
3. **Heat offtake feasibility** — which public buildings, homes, greenhouses, or other users can absorb useful server heat.
4. **Fiber feasibility** — whether reliable backhaul can support black-box compute tenancy and service-level commitments.
5. **Community process** — how the project should proceed through FPIC, local governance, benefits, jobs, and heat allocation.
6. **Pilot economics** — whether a first pad and heat loop can be financed, permitted, built, operated, and expanded responsibly.

Kristal Farms is not asking partners to accept unresolved risks blindly. The requested first step is a structured partner review leading to a clear go/no-go decision for the pilot validation phase.

## 3. Partner types

Kristal Farms may require several partner categories. A single organization may fill more than one role, but the roles should be kept clear.

### 3.1 Hydro / utility partner

A hydro or utility partner helps validate and structure the power side of the project.

**Potential partner profile:** local utility, hydro operator, grid operator, energy authority, or power systems engineering partner.

**What Kristal Farms needs from this partner:**

- Local hydro resource and power availability review.
- Interconnection concept for a short medium-voltage feed to the village or pad yard.
- Village substation requirements.
- Power quality requirements for compute loads.
- Metering framework for power delivered to pads, heat systems, and local/community uses.
- Protection, grounding, sequencing, and outage protocols.
- Confirmation that local loads and essential services remain protected.
- Input on diesel displacement and backup strategy.

**What the partner may receive:**

- New local offtake for renewable power.
- A staged, metered demand source that can scale by pads.
- Infrastructure investment linked to community heat and connectivity benefits.
- A replicable coastal-community model if the first site is validated.

**Immediate decision needed:**

Can the partner support a preliminary power and interconnection review for Nain as the first target site?

---

### 3.2 Infrastructure investor

An infrastructure investor helps finance the development path from concept to pilot and from pilot to expansion pads.

**Potential partner profile:** infrastructure fund, impact investor, climate infrastructure investor, community infrastructure fund, strategic energy investor, or regional development capital provider.

**What Kristal Farms needs from this partner:**

- Capital for Phase 0 and Phase 1 diligence.
- Capital structuring for pilot pad development.
- Financing model for compute pads, heat loop, fiber upgrades, port logistics, and operations.
- Risk allocation input.
- Governance expectations.
- Discipline around what can be shared before and after NDA.

**What the partner may receive:**

- Exposure to clean-energy-backed compute infrastructure.
- A phased project that can scale by modular pad additions rather than a single megaproject.
- ESG-linked community value through heat reuse, local jobs, training, and diesel displacement.
- Potential participation in the pilot SPV or future expansion SPVs.

**Immediate decision needed:**

Is the investor interested in funding or co-funding a defined pre-feasibility package covering site, power, fiber, heat offtake, logistics, permitting, and community process?

---

### 3.3 Compute tenant

A compute tenant brings demand for secure, power-dense compute capacity.

**Potential partner profile:** AI company, cloud provider, research compute user, university consortium, high-performance computing user, enterprise compute buyer, or operator of tenant-owned hardware.

**What Kristal Farms needs from this partner:**

- Target power density and compute load profile.
- Hardware ownership model: tenant-owned, supplier-owned, or hosted infrastructure.
- Cooling interface requirements.
- Fiber capacity and latency expectations.
- Uptime, maintenance, and remote-hands requirements.
- Security, privacy, and optional hardware-attestation requirements.
- Minimum viable pad size and acceptable expansion pathway.

**What the partner may receive:**

- Renewable-power-backed compute pad tenancy.
- Physical infrastructure services: power, cooling interface, fiber handoff, heat export, physical security, and metering.
- Black-box operating model where the host does not access tenant data, logs, models, applications, or packet payloads.
- Potential ESG value from heat reuse and community benefit reporting.

**Immediate decision needed:**

Can the tenant provide a non-binding technical requirements profile for a first pilot pad?

---

### 3.4 Modular data center supplier

A modular data center supplier helps turn the pad concept into buildable infrastructure.

**Potential partner profile:** containerized data center vendor, cooling-system integrator, liquid-cooling supplier, electrical systems integrator, EPC contractor, or remote-site operations provider.

**What Kristal Farms needs from this partner:**

- Container or modular data hall design options.
- Electrical intake and distribution requirements.
- Cooling interface design.
- Heat capture interface design.
- Winterization and coastal-weather hardening.
- Fire detection and suppression concept.
- Remote monitoring requirements.
- Maintenance model for Labrador coastal logistics.
- Construction schedule and sealift constraints.

**What the partner may receive:**

- A defined use case for deployable, heat-integrated modular compute.
- Potential pilot-supplier role.
- Opportunity to establish a repeatable coastal deployment template.
- Long-term expansion opportunities if the Nain pilot validates.

**Immediate decision needed:**

Can the supplier provide a non-binding concept package for one pilot pad, including electrical, cooling, heat, logistics, and operations assumptions?

---

### 3.5 Telecom / fiber partner

A telecom or fiber partner makes the core operating model possible: Kristal Farms exports compute results by data connection, not electricity by long transmission line.

**Potential partner profile:** telecom carrier, fiber owner, network operator, subsea/terrestrial fiber developer, regional broadband partner, or network engineering firm.

**What Kristal Farms needs from this partner:**

- Existing fiber and backhaul review.
- Route options from the community to a regional hub.
- Redundancy and path-protection concept.
- NOC architecture input.
- Pad-level dual uplink requirements.
- SLA assumptions for availability, latency, jitter, and restoration.
- Separation of tenant traffic from community traffic.
- Security model consistent with black-box tenancy.

**What the partner may receive:**

- Anchor demand for improved regional connectivity.
- A route justification linked to compute, public services, education, health, and community broadband.
- Participation in a replicable coastal fiber-plus-compute model.

**Immediate decision needed:**

Can the partner support a preliminary connectivity and route-feasibility review for Nain and future coastal replication sites?

---

### 3.6 Community / Indigenous / government partner

A community or government partner is essential because the project only works if it produces local value and proceeds through legitimate governance.

**Potential partner profile:** Inuit community government, Indigenous development corporation, Nunatsiavut institution, municipal/local authority, provincial or federal government agency, regional economic development body, housing/public works agency, or public-building owner.

**What Kristal Farms needs from this partner:**

- Guidance on whether and how the project should enter local discussion.
- FPIC process expectations.
- Local priorities for heat use: public buildings, homes, greenhouse, clinic, school, community facilities, or other users.
- Land and siting constraints.
- Local hiring and training priorities.
- Environmental and cultural constraints.
- Governance expectations for benefits, heat allocation, monitoring, and grievance process.
- Requirements for public transparency.

**What the partner may receive:**

- Local heat value from server waste heat.
- Potential diesel displacement.
- Local jobs, training, and operations roles.
- Better connectivity where fiber upgrades are feasible.
- Community benefit structures tied to measurable project performance.
- A stronger local role in how energy, heat, land, and digital infrastructure are used.

**Immediate decision needed:**

Is the community or public partner open to a structured, non-committal project introduction and scoping process for Nain?

## 4. What Kristal Farms brings

Kristal Farms brings the integrated project concept and coordination layer.

### 4.1 Project thesis

Kristal Farms brings a clear thesis:

> Use cold local hydro for modular compute, place containers near village heat users, export compute by fiber, recycle server heat locally, operate black-box tenancy, and scale by coastal replication rather than inland megaprojects.

### 4.2 Integrated architecture

Kristal Farms brings the system architecture connecting:

- Local hydro integration.
- Short medium-voltage connection.
- Village substation.
- Modular compute pad yard.
- Containerized compute tenancy.
- Cooling and heat recovery interface.
- Village heat loop.
- Greenhouse or seasonal heat sink.
- Fiber backhaul and NOC.
- Public metrics and audit framework.

### 4.3 Partner coordination

Kristal Farms acts as the integrator across power, compute, fiber, heat, capital, governance, and community value. Each partner should have a clear role, and no partner should be asked to own risks outside its mandate.

### 4.4 Black-box tenancy model

Kristal Farms brings a tenancy model where the host provides infrastructure services up to the pad boundary, while the tenant controls its internal hardware, software, models, workloads, data, and security environment.

Host-visible metrics are limited to physical and service metrics such as power, heat, cooling flow and temperature, fiber availability, bandwidth volume, uptime, and alarms. The host does not inspect tenant content.

### 4.5 Heat-first operating logic

Kristal Farms brings a non-negotiable heat hierarchy:

1. Reuse heat locally.
2. Store heat when useful.
3. Reject remaining heat only when needed and permitted.

The purpose is to make compute infrastructure produce visible local value rather than only remote digital value.

### 4.6 Labrador coast focus

Kristal Farms brings a focused site logic: Nain first, then other Labrador coastal communities if the pilot validates. The project is not currently positioned as a Churchill Falls megaproject, inland transmission project, or broad Nunavik rollout.

## 6. Preferred partnership structures

The exact structure should be selected after partner alignment and early diligence. The following structures are preferred starting points.

### 6.1 Development MOU

A non-binding agreement for early partner alignment.

**Use when:** partners want to explore the opportunity without committing capital or final approvals.

**Typical scope:** information sharing, workplan, roles, confidentiality, decision schedule, and diligence responsibilities.

### 6.2 NDA and data room access

A controlled information-sharing process.

**Use when:** partners need access to technical, site, commercial, map, risk, or governance materials.

**Typical scope:** confidentiality, permitted uses, distribution limits, source hierarchy, and version control.

### 6.3 Site and power due diligence agreement

A focused agreement for hydro, interconnection, site, and village-load validation.

**Use when:** the utility, community, and technical partners are ready to test whether Nain can host a pilot.

**Typical scope:** power study, site walkdown, heat-load survey, fiber review, logistics review, permitting scan, and budget.

### 6.4 Pilot SPV or joint development vehicle

A project vehicle for the first pad and related heat/fiber infrastructure.

**Use when:** site validation, community process, and anchor partner interest are strong enough to justify pilot development.

**Typical scope:** ownership, governance, capital contributions, development rights, procurement, operating model, and expansion rights.

### 6.5 Pad lease and SLA

A commercial agreement between the host and compute tenant.

**Use when:** a tenant is ready to lease a prepared pad or container position.

**Typical scope:** power cap, cooling interface, heat export, fiber handoff, uptime, maintenance windows, metering, physical access, security, insurance, and reversibility.

### 6.6 Equipment supply / EPC agreement

A delivery agreement for container, electrical, cooling, heat, and control systems.

**Use when:** technical specifications are mature enough for procurement.

**Typical scope:** design responsibility, factory acceptance testing, delivery, installation, commissioning, warranties, spare parts, remote support, and training.

### 6.7 Fiber service, IRU, or build agreement

A connectivity agreement with the telecom partner.

**Use when:** route, capacity, redundancy, and service model are defined.

**Typical scope:** capacity, uptime, latency assumptions, restoration, path diversity, community-service provisions, and NOC responsibilities.

### 6.8 Community Benefits Agreement / IBA-style agreement

A community-centered agreement that defines benefits, governance, employment, training, heat allocation, monitoring, grievance process, and reporting.

**Use when:** the project has entered a formal community process and the parties are defining the conditions under which it may proceed.

**Typical scope:** FPIC process, benefit-sharing, local jobs, training, heat priorities, committee structure, environmental safeguards, reporting, and dispute process.

### 6.9 Heat offtake agreement

An agreement with public buildings, housing operators, greenhouse operators, or other heat users.

**Use when:** actual heat loads and connection costs have been measured.

**Typical scope:** supply temperature, metering, backup heat responsibility, maintenance, seasonal priorities, pricing or credit structure, and service limits.

## 7. Immediate decision points

Partners do not need to decide everything now. The immediate decision points are narrow.

### Gate A — Role confirmation

Each partner should confirm which role it is evaluating:

- Power / utility.
- Capital / investor.
- Compute tenant.
- Modular supplier.
- Fiber / telecom.
- Community / government.
- Heat user.
- Other.

### Gate B — Information access

Partners should decide whether they need NDA-protected data room access.

Likely NDA-only materials include:

- Detailed maps and site files.
- Technical assumptions.
- Commercial model detail.
- Risk register.
- Partner-specific deal structures.
- Unvalidated financial scenarios.

### Gate C — First-site alignment

Partners should confirm whether they accept the working focus:

> Nain is the first target. Labrador coastal replication is the expansion logic.

This does not mean Nain is approved. It means Nain is the first site to validate.

### Gate D — Diligence workplan

Partners should agree on the first diligence workplan:

- Site and land constraints.
- Hydro and interconnection review.
- Village heat-load survey.
- Fiber route and redundancy review.
- Port, sealift, and logistics review.
- Environmental and permitting scan.
- Community process design.
- Pilot pad technical concept.
- Pilot budget range.

### Gate E — Pilot validation decision

After diligence, partners decide whether to proceed to pilot design.

The pilot should proceed only if the following are credible:

- Community process is legitimate and active.
- Power supply and interconnection path are feasible.
- Fiber path is feasible.
- Heat users are identified.
- Pilot pad can be delivered and operated safely.
- Risk allocation is acceptable.
- At least one credible compute demand path exists.
- Benefits and reporting can be defined transparently.

## 8. What Kristal Farms is not asking for yet

Kristal Farms is not asking partners for:

- Final investment approval.
- Final construction approval.
- A signed long-term power purchase agreement.
- Land transfer or permanent site control.
- Final community consent before a proper process.
- Acceptance of unvalidated financial projections.
- Disclosure of tenant data, models, logs, or packet contents.
- Commitment to inland megaprojects or long high-voltage transmission projects.

The current ask is to enter a disciplined partner-alignment and validation process.

---

## 9. Partner fit checklist

A partner is likely a strong fit if it can answer “yes” to at least one of the following:

- Can you help validate or supply local renewable power?
- Can you help finance a pilot pad and heat loop?
- Can you bring credible compute demand?
- Can you supply rugged modular compute infrastructure?
- Can you provide fiber, backhaul, redundancy, or network operations support?
- Can you help define community benefits, governance, heat allocation, jobs, and training?
- Can you help validate environmental, permitting, logistics, or operating risks?

A partner is not a strong fit if it requires the project to abandon the core thesis: Labrador coast focus, village-first heat reuse, black-box tenancy, community value, and staged modular replication.

## 10. Proposed next meeting agenda

A first partner meeting should be short and decision-oriented.

### Agenda

1. Confirm partner type and role.
2. Confirm whether Nain-first / Labrador-coast framing is understood.
3. Review what Kristal Farms brings.
4. Review what the partner may bring.
5. Identify missing diligence items.
6. Decide whether NDA/data room access is needed.
7. Agree on immediate next step and responsible contact.

### Desired outcome

The desired outcome is one of three decisions:

1. **Proceed to diligence** — partner wants to participate in early validation.
2. **Hold for later** — partner may be relevant after site, power, fiber, or community process is clearer.
3. **Not a fit** — partner does not match the current Labrador coast model.

---

## 11. Summary ask

Kristal Farms is seeking partners who can help validate and build a first Labrador coastal pilot, beginning with Nain.

The project does not need every partner to do everything. It needs each partner to own a clear role:

- Power partners validate energy and interconnection.
- Investors validate capital and risk.
- Compute tenants validate demand and SLA needs.
- Modular suppliers validate buildability.
- Fiber partners validate connectivity.
- Community and government partners define legitimacy, benefits, governance, and local value.

The near-term ask is simple:

> Confirm your role, enter the appropriate information-sharing process, and participate in a focused diligence workplan to determine whether Nain can become the first Kristal Farms pilot site.
